# Hyperion v1.3: drive stage research (DRV8874 and alternatives)

Status: research note, 2026-10-31. Nothing in `hardware/` has been changed. JLCPCB stock and prices are
a snapshot from that date, taken with the pcbparts JLC tools. Prices are USD per unit.

## 1. Summary

**Recommendation: replace both DRV8231A with the TI DRV8874PWPR (LCSC C1855818).** Feed both VREF pins
from GPIO4 (PWM slice 2A) through 10 kΩ, with 100 kΩ to GND and 1 µF. Keep R_IPROPI = 1.5 kΩ 1 %, the
same part as v1.2 (C22843).

- The current limit can be set from firmware anywhere from 0 to 4.44 A. A duty of 67.5 % gives 3 A and
  90 % gives 4 A. The v1.2 hardware ceiling is 1.47 A.
- The DRV8874 is cheaper than the DRV8231A ($0.88 vs $1.06 at 10+), has 15.3k in stock, and is pin
  compatible with the DRV8876. A cheaper, lower-current BOM variant can use the same footprint.
- Thermal: 3 A continuous on both channels **cannot** be held in steady state by any candidate on a
  34 × 28 mm board. The DRV8874 runs about 100–120 s at 3 A on both channels before reaching 150 °C
  junction. That covers real antweight pushing bouts. At 3 A it heats the board about as much as v1.2
  does at its 1.47 A limit, and its junction runs cooler. Section 6 has the numbers.
- The VREF filter values are fine as proposed: τ = 9.1 ms, ripple 2.3 mV p-p at 36.6 kHz, which is
  about 3 mA of current-limit ripple. **However, VREF = 0 V does not reliably mean "motors off".** Make
  nSLEEP the hard off and route it to a GPIO. Section 4.4 explains why.
- Move v1.3 to 4 layers with a solid GND plane on L2. The DRV8874 needs it to shed heat. TI's 2-layer
  thermal data is much worse at small copper areas.

## 2. Why the DRV8231A runs out

From the DRV8231A datasheet (SLVSFZ8A, §6.3 and §6.5):

- A_IPROPI = 1500 µA/A, and I_IPROPI is limited to 3 mA. That puts the regulation ceiling at
  3 mA / 1500 µA/A = **2.0 A**, whatever the VREF and R_IPROPI values.
- v1.2 has VREF = 3.3 V and R_IPROPI = 1.5 kΩ, so I_TRIP = 3.3 / (1500 µA/A × 1500 Ω) = **1.47 A**.
  This checks out.
- R_DS(on) is 300 + 300 mΩ typical, and RθJA is 66.5 °C/W (DSG package). At 1.47 A the driver already
  dissipates about 1.8 W at temperature (section 6).

Notes:

- **DRV8874 vs DRV8876.** Same die family and same pinout (DRV8874 datasheet §1, "pin to pin R_DS(on)
  variants"). A_IPROPI differs: 450 vs 1000 µA/A. A DRV8876 build therefore needs R_IPROPI of about
  680 Ω to keep the same scaling.
- **DRV8251A.** Its peak current looks attractive, but 450 mΩ at 3 A is 4 W or more (section 6). Its
  IPROPI ceiling, 3 mA / 1575 µA/A ≈ 1.9 A, is no better than the DRV8231A. Not a 3 A part.
- **DRV8256P.** This part takes VREF directly, so the PWM/RC idea works without an IPROPI resistor.
  The cost is current telemetry, which v1.2 has and the README promises. The VREF input also leaks up to
  8.25 µA (§6.5). Through the 9.1 kΩ Thévenin resistance that is 75 mV, or about 115 mA of limit error.
  The minimum specified VREF is 0.05 V. The part is large (HTSSOP-28) and has low JLC stock.
- **DRV8243H-Q1.** Thermally best by far. But the trip threshold is a fixed level chosen by a pin, so
  a PWM-set VREF is impossible. The limit can only change with R_IPROPI or through the SPI variant
  (C5218846, 2 in stock). IPROPI clamps at 4.5–5.5 V, above the RP2354A ADC's 3.3 V range, so it needs
  a series resistor and clamp on each IPROPI line. Price $6.27, 218 in stock.
- **DRV8873H.** In the H variant the I_TRIP threshold is fixed at ≥ 3.27 A minimum (§6.5), so it cannot
  set a 1–3 A limit. Not suitable.
- **AT8236 and other Chinese "8874-class" clones.** No DRV8874 pin-compatible clone turned up in the JLC
  catalogue: `jlc_stock_check "8874"` returns only TI parts, and `jlc_find_alternatives` for C1855818
  found no verified alternative. The AT8236 (C2827823, 61,995 in stock, $0.51) is the closest on paper.
  Its LCSC datasheet could not be fetched as text from here. Other "AT8236" parts (MSKSEMI AT8236-MS,
  TECH PUBLIC AT8236-TP) are DRV8870-style: VREF plus an ISEN shunt, 3.6 A, 260 mΩ. If the Zhongkewei
  part also regulates through an external shunt, then 3 A at a typical 0.1 Ω adds 0.9 W in the shunt,
  which cancels its R_DS(on) advantage. Its temperature range is also only −40…85 °C. Not recommended
  without a bench test.
- **RZ7899 family.** These are toy-grade drivers: no exposed pad, no adjustable regulation, and VM
  ratings with no margin over 4S plus regen spikes. Rejected.

## 4. VREF from PWM: filter check

The proposed circuit is GPIO4 (PWM2A) → 10 kΩ → VREF node → 100 kΩ to GND, with 1 µF from VREF to GND.
The node feeds both DRV8874 VREF pins.

### 4.1 DC transfer

- Divider ratio k = 100k / 110k = 0.909. With 3.3 V IOVDD, VREF_max = 3.00 V.
- VREF = D × 3.00 V. This is inside the DRV8874 VREF range of 0–3.6 V (§6.3). Absolute maximum is 5.75 V.
- Thévenin resistance R_th = 10k ∥ 100k = 9.09 kΩ. GPIO load is 30 µA at most, which is negligible.
- The DRV8874 datasheet gives no VREF input current. It is a comparator input, so assume it is small.
  The DRV8256 is the exception at 8.25 µA (see above).

### 4.2 Time constant and settling

τ = R_th × C = 9.09 kΩ × 1 µF = **9.09 ms**.

| Settling | Time |
|---|---|
| 10–90 % | 20.0 ms |
| 1 % | 41.9 ms |
| 0.1 % | 62.8 ms |

This suits a current limit that firmware changes rarely, for example a per-match setting, a stall
derate or thermal foldback. If firmware needs to switch the limit within a few ms, for example a short
"boost", use 220 nF (τ = 2.0 ms). The ripple table below shows that cost.

### 4.3 PWM frequency and ripple

Set RP2354A clk_sys to 150 MHz (the default), slice 2, clkdiv 1, TOP = 4095. This gives
**f = 36.6 kHz** with 12-bit resolution, or 0.73 mV per step. With R_IPROPI = 1.5 kΩ, one step is about
1.1 mA of current limit.

Exact peak-to-peak ripple for a 1st-order RC at D = 0.5 (the worst case):

| PWM (TOP) | 1 µF (τ 9.09 ms) | 220 nF (τ 2.0 ms) | 100 nF (τ 0.91 ms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 36.6 kHz (4095) | 2.25 mV (3.3 mA) | 10.2 mV (15 mA) | 22.5 mV (33 mA) |
| 73.2 kHz (2047) | 1.13 mV | 5.1 mV | 11.3 mV |
| 146.5 kHz (1023) | 0.56 mV | 2.6 mV | 5.6 mV |

The values in brackets convert ripple to I_TRIP ripple with R_IPROPI = 1.5 kΩ (1 mV ≈ 1.48 mA). Every
option is negligible next to the ±5.5 % A_IPROPI error. **1 µF at 36.6 kHz is fine.** Use a 0402 X5R
part rated 10 V or more. The 25 V basic part C52923 has little DC-bias loss at 3 V.

The firmware duty formula is D = I_target × 450 µA/A × R_IPROPI / 3.00 V. Clamp D in firmware to the
highest limit you want to allow.

### 4.4 Boot state and the "VREF = 0 V means motors off" assumption

1. **Before firmware runs**, GPIO4 is not driven: its function select is NULL at reset and the pad has
   its default pull-down. The 100 kΩ pulls VREF to 0 V, with τ = 9 ms decay from any earlier value.
   This holds through reset, the bootrom and the USB BOOTSEL/UF2 mode.
2. **RP2350 erratum E9.** On the original A2 stepping, an input pad with its input buffer enabled can
   leak about 120 µA and sit near 2.2 V. A pull-down needs to be 8.2 kΩ or less to beat it. On an A2
   chip, GPIO4 could therefore float to about 2.2 V, giving VREF ≈ 2.0 V, which is about 3 A of limit
   at boot. Raspberry Pi fixed E9 in A3/A4, and the RP2354 launched with the A4 stepping. Hyperion
   should be safe, but check the marking on the received chips. **The 100 kΩ pull-down would not
   survive E9.**
3. **VREF = 0 V is not a guaranteed off.** The datasheet allows VREF = 0 V (§6.3) but does not describe
   output behaviour at I_TRIP = 0. In fixed off-time mode, the bridge turns on according to IN1/IN2. The
   comparator can only act after t_BLK + t_DEG (1.1 + 0.6 µs, §6.5), and the bridge then brakes for
   t_OFF = 25 µs (§7.3.3.2.1). So expect drive bursts of about 1.7 µs every 27 µs (about 6 % duty)
   while IN1/IN2 command drive. The low-current offset error is ±30 mA below 0.4 A (§6.5), so the exact
   trip point near zero is undefined. The result is low torque, not off. Also, the IPROPI clamp follows
   VREF (§7.3.3.1), so current telemetry reads zero while VREF = 0.
4. **Use nSLEEP as the hard off.** nSLEEP has an internal 100 kΩ pull-down (R_PD, §6.5), so the drivers
   sleep with outputs Hi-Z until firmware raises it. IN1/IN2 also have internal pull-downs, which give
   coast. Route both nSLEEP pins to one free GPIO. Raise nSLEEP only after RC failsafe is cleared and
   VREF is set. After raising it, wait t_WAKE = 1 ms. IMODE is latched on the nSLEEP rising edge (§7.3.3.2).
   With this, VREF = 0 becomes a second layer of protection, which is how it should be treated.

## 5. R_IPROPI choice for 3 A and 4 A (DRV8874)

DRV8874 §7.3.3.2, Eq. 3: I_TRIP = V_VREF / (A_IPROPI × R_IPROPI), with A_IPROPI = 450 µA/A typical.
The IPROPI current is limited to 3 mA (§6.3), which puts the regulation ceiling at 6.67 A. The
overcurrent trip I_OCP is 6 A minimum, 10 A typical (§6.5). VREF_max = 3.00 V from the divider.

| R_IPROPI (1 %) | I_TRIP at 100 % duty | Duty for 3 A | Duty for 4 A | ADC resolution (12-bit, 3.3 V) | I_IPROPI at max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.5 kΩ (C22843, reused from v1.2)** | **4.44 A** | **67.5 %** | **90.0 %** | 1.19 mA/LSB | 2.0 mA |
| 1.65 kΩ | 4.04 A | 74.2 % | 99.0 % | 1.09 mA/LSB | 1.8 mA |
| 2.0 kΩ | 3.33 A | 90.0 % | not reachable | 0.90 mA/LSB | 1.5 mA |
| 2.2 kΩ | 3.03 A | 99.0 % | not reachable | 0.81 mA/LSB | 1.4 mA |

Tolerance stack, worst case: A_ERR ±5.5 % (2–4 A, §6.5), R_IPROPI ±1 %, ME6211 3V3 ±2 % and divider
ratio about ±0.2 %.

- Programmed 3 A gives **2.75–3.28 A**.
- Programmed 4 A gives **3.67–4.37 A**.
- The 100 % duty ceiling with 1.5 kΩ is at most 4.85 A, still below I_OCP(min) = 6 A.

**Use 1.5 kΩ.** It reaches 4 A with headroom for tolerance and reuses an existing BOM line. IPROPI then
peaks at 3.0 V because it clamps to VREF, which suits the RP2354A ADC. If you want a hardware ceiling
that a firmware bug cannot exceed, 1.65 kΩ caps the limit at about 4.0 A typical, but the 4 A setting
then needs 99 % duty and has no tolerance headroom.

The 10 nF IPROPI capacitor option (§7.3.3.2) only matters if false trips appear from motor-capacitor
transients. With 1.5 kΩ it adds a 15 µs time constant, which is comparable to t_OFF. Place a DNP 0402
footprint and leave it unfitted.

## 6. Thermal estimate at 3 A

### Method

Losses are P = I²·R_DS(on)(T_J) + V_M·I·t_edge·f_PWM + V_M·I_VM. This is DRV8874 §8.2.1.2.2, Eq. 8–18.
Conditions: V_M = 16.8 V, I = 3 A, 20 kHz drive PWM, ambient inside the robot T_A = 35 °C.

R_DS(on) rises with temperature as the datasheets show:

- DRV8874: about 1.35× from 25 to 150 °C (Fig. 6/7).
- DRV8256: 82 → 125 mΩ from 25 to 125 °C.
- DRV8243: 42 → 80 mΩ maximum at 150 °C.

Three views:

1. **Vendor RθJA.** JEDEC-style large board. This is optimistic.
2. **Hyperion, one channel at 3 A, steady state.** The 34 × 28 mm board is a single lump with
   R_board-amb ≈ 45 °C/W. That figure comes from 19 cm² of both sides at h ≈ 12 W/m²K in still, enclosed
   air. The second table uses 30 °C/W for some airflow. Junction temperature = board temperature +
   P × Ψ_JB. Other board losses add 0.3 W.
3. **Hyperion, both channels at 3 A continuous, transient.** Board heat capacity is about 5 J/K: FR-4
   about 3.4 J/K, four copper layers about 0.5 J/K, parts about 1 J/K. That gives a board time constant
   of about 3–4 minutes, roughly one match.

The model is lumped and should be read as ±30 %. It ranks the parts, but measure the real board before
trusting the absolute numbers.

R_board-amb = 45 °C/W:

| Part | Loss at 3 A, T_J ≈ 110 °C | T_J, vendor RθJA, 1 ch | T_J, Hyperion, 1 ch steady | Board heating, 2 ch | Time to T_J = 150 °C, 2 ch at 3 A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DRV8231A **at 1.47 A** (v1.2 today) | 1.81 W | 175 °C | 232 °C (TSD) | 0.78 °C/s | 73 s |
| **DRV8874** | 2.43 W | 125 °C | 214 °C (TSD) | 1.03 °C/s | **104 s** |
| DRV8256 PWP | 2.25 W | 99 °C | 214 °C | 0.96 °C/s | 126 s |
| DRV8243H VQFN-HR | 1.30 W | 95 °C | 119 °C | 0.58 °C/s | 339 s |
| DRV8873H | 2.04 W | 89 °C | 166 °C | 0.88 °C/s | 168 s |
| AT8236 (claimed R_DS(on), assumed package) | 2.58 W | 171 °C | 274 °C | 1.09 °C/s | 77 s |
| DRV8251A | 5.63 W | runaway | TSD | 2.31 °C/s | 10 s |
| DRV8876 / DRV8231A at 3 A | 7–8 W | runaway | TSD | about 3 °C/s | immediate |

R_board-amb = 30 °C/W, with airflow or heat sinking into the chassis:

| Part | T_J, Hyperion, 1 ch steady | Time to 150 °C, 2 ch |
|---|---|---|
| DRV8231A at 1.47 A | 176 °C | 80 s |
| DRV8874 | 152 °C | 120 s |
| DRV8256 PWP | 142 °C | 149 s |
| DRV8243H | 91 °C | never |
| DRV8873H | 117 °C | 220 s |

What this means:

- On this board, the board itself limits heat, not the package. Every part with R_DS(on) ≥ 150 mΩ is
  transient-limited at 3 A continuous. Only the DRV8243 (84 mΩ) gets near steady state.
- **The DRV8874 at 3 A puts the board about where v1.2 already is at 1.47 A,** and its junction runs
  cooler: Ψ_JB 11 °C/W against 32.7 °C/W for the WSON. v1.3 doubles the usable current without a worse
  thermal design point than v1.2.
- TI's own 4-layer simulation (DRV8874 §8.2.1.2.3, Fig. 21) gives RθJA of about 36 °C/W at 2 cm² top
  copper, falling to about 26 °C/W at 16 cm². That model has a 74 × 74 mm inner GND plane, about six
  times Hyperion's area, so treat it as an upper bound on cooling. The 2-layer curves (Fig. 23) are
  several times worse at small copper areas. This is the case for going to 4 layers.
- Transient headroom is large. Fig. 25/26 show Z_θJA of only a few °C/W for 1 s pulses, so stall
  spikes of several amps for under a second are fine.
- Firmware should use the PWM VREF for thermal foldback. Integrate I² from IPROPI, or read a board NTC,
  and lower VREF before TSD trips. The DRV8874 TSD is 160 °C minimum with auto-recovery (§6.5). A TSD
  mid-push drops the drive for both motors.

## 7. Layout impact vs v1.2

### Area

Approximate courtyards:

| | v1.2 DRV8231A | v1.3 DRV8874 |
|---|---|---|
| Package | WSON-8, 2 × 2 mm | HTSSOP-16, 5.0 × 4.4 mm body, 6.4 mm over leads |
| Courtyard per driver | about 8 mm² | about 38 mm² (5.5 × 6.9 mm) |
| Two drivers | about 16 mm² | about 76 mm² (**+60 mm²**, about 6 % of the 952 mm² board) |

For comparison: DRV8243 VQFN-HR about 18 mm², DRV8256 HTSSOP-28 about 70 mm², DRV8256 VQFN-24 about
20 mm². The DRV8874 pinout is easy to route. Inputs and analog pins are on one side (pins 1–7).
OUT1/PGND/OUT2/VM and the charge pump are on the other side (pins 8–14), and the motor pads are at the
board edge.

Pin straps, which need no parts:

- PMODE tied to 3V3 selects PWM (IN/IN) mode, the same interface as the DRV8231A, so firmware does
  not change (§7.3.2.2).
- IMODE tied to GND selects fixed off-time and auto-retry OCP (Table 6).
- nFAULT goes to a GPIO with the internal pull-up, wired-OR for both drivers, or is left NC.
- nSLEEP goes to one GPIO, shared by both drivers (section 4.4).

That is 7 new placed parts and 1 or 2 new GPIOs: nSLEEP, plus nFAULT if wanted. The schematic already
has `VREF`, `MA_IPROPI` and `MB_IPROPI` nets, so the IPROPI-to-ADC routing carries over.

### Bulk and decoupling

Keep v1.2's 100 nF and 10 µF X7R 1206 per driver, placed tight to pin 11 (VM) and pin 9 (PGND). At
16.8 V a 50 V 1206 10 µF keeps only about half its capacitance. With twice the motor current, consider
a second 10 µF per driver, and treat the hand-wired 100 µF (C3) as needed rather than optional. TI
§9.1 gives no fixed minimum. Size it from measured VM ripple.

### Thermal layout

- Connect the exposed pad to GND with the via grid from the PWP land pattern (datasheet §12 mechanical
  pages).
- Use a solid GND plane on L2. Pour GND copper around the drivers on top and bottom.
- Keep VM, OUTx and PGND traces wide (§10.1).
- Do not put the RP2354A or the IMU next to the drivers. The LSM6DSV32X has an offset drift with
  temperature.

## 8. Final ranking

1. **DRV8874PWPR, C1855818.** 15,284 in stock, extended part, $1.013 / $0.878 (10+) / $0.659 (100+).
   It meets every requirement: 3–4 A programmable limit via PWM VREF, IPROPI telemetry, 37 V, an
   easy-to-assemble HTSSOP, and a cost below v1.2. It is the only candidate that does all of this. Its
   limit is board heat during long two-motor pushes, which firmware foldback handles.
2. **DRV8243HQRXYRQ1, C3040833.** 218 in stock, extended part, $6.27. Choose it only if testing shows
   long 3 A pushes on both motors at once. It has about half the losses. Costs: no PWM-VREF (the limit
   is a fixed level times R_IPROPI), IPROPI needs clamping for the ADC, VQFN-HR is harder to rework,
   and there are about 6× the cost and thin stock.
3. **DRV8256PPWPR, C3681352.** 259 in stock, extended part, $3.76. It is the only other part with a
   true analog VREF limit (I_TRIP = VREF / 0.66). Costs: no current telemetry, a large HTSSOP-28, 8 µA
   VREF leakage, and low stock. The VQFN-24 version (DRV8256PRGER, C5218854) has only 12 in stock.

Not recommended for a 3 A channel: DRV8876 (C575551), DRV8251A (C5218844), DRV8873H (C2150604), and
AT8236 (C2827823) until bench-verified. The RZ7899 family and DRV8212/DRV8213 are out.

## 9. Open items to check before layout

- Confirm the RP2354A stepping (A4) on received parts (E9, section 4.4).
- Confirm that GPIO4 and one GPIO for nSLEEP are free in `mcu.kicad_sch`. If GPIO5 (PWM2B) is also
  free, it can drive a second VREF network to give each channel its own limit.
- Bench test at VREF = 0 with IN1/IN2 driven, to confirm the burst behaviour in section 4.4.
- Measure board temperature rise at 3 A, one and two channels, to calibrate the 45 °C/W assumption.
- DRV8874 VREF input current is not specified. Measure the VREF node at a few duty values.
